# Jen Practice Sheet - A Minor Pentatonic Consolidation

Date: 2026-07-16

Practice commitment: 20 minutes a day

Main goal: make A minor pentatonic feel musical, familiar, and useful for jamming.

## The Simple Rule

Do not practise everything at once.

Each day:

1. Tune in.
2. Repeat the A minor pentatonic map.
3. Make one small musical phrase.
4. End with jamming or a song idea.

## Daily 20-Minute Practice

### 1. Tune In - 2 minutes

- Tune the guitar.
- Check shoulders, jaw, breath, left hand, and right hand.
- Play open strings slowly.
- Listen for clean sound.

Reminder: clean and calm matters more than fast.

### 2. A Root Notes - 4 minutes

Practise finding A as home.

- Find A on the low E string.
- Find A on the A string.
- Find A on the top strings if remembered.
- Play A, pause, listen.
- Make A sound musical with rhythm.

Goal: A should feel like the safe note you can always return to.

### 3. A Minor Pentatonic Map - 5 minutes

Practise the A minor pentatonic slowly.

- Use the current shape/box you know.
- Say the root note when you land on A.
- Do not rush.
- If the full shape feels messy, use only two strings.
- If two strings feel good, add one more string.

Goal: the scale should become a map, not a blur.

### 4. Right-Hand Pattern - 4 minutes

Choose one right-hand pattern and repeat it.

- Keep the pulse steady.
- Stay relaxed.
- Try it over A minor or an A note.
- If it gets messy, slow down.

Goal: the right hand should make the scale feel like music.

### 5. Tiny Jam - 5 minutes

Use the scale musically.

- Play only A roots first.
- Add one nearby pentatonic note.
- Add one more note.
- Make a tiny phrase.
- Repeat it.
- Change the ending.

Goal: make one small phrase you would actually want to hear again.

## Extra Drills To Rotate

Use these only when you have energy. Do not force all of them every day.

### E and A String Landmarks

- Practise note locations on the E string.
- Practise note locations on the A string.
- Say the note name out loud.
- Go slowly.

### A / E / C / D On The Top Strings

- Find A, E, C, and D on the top two strings.
- Play them slowly.
- Say the names.
- Make two-note patterns.

### Marcello-Style Scale Warm-Up

- Play slowly.
- Aim for clean sound.
- Do not chase speed.
- Stop if tension appears.

### Finger-Independence Drill

- Keep this tiny.
- Move one finger at a time.
- Stay relaxed.
- Stop before frustration.

### Gentle Flamenco Feel

- Use this for energy and rhythm.
- Keep it playful.
- Do not worry about perfection yet.

## CAGED Clue

CAGED helped, but it should stay simple for now.

Use this sentence:

> Chord shapes are landmarks. Pentatonic notes live around those landmarks.

For now, do not learn the full CAGED system. Just notice that chord shapes and scale notes belong on the same guitar map.

## What To Notice

After practice, answer one or two:

- What felt like home?
- What sounded musical?
- What felt confusing?
- What needs repetition?
- What phrase did I like?
