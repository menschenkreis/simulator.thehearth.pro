# Jen Lesson Teacher Pack

Date: 2026-06-25

## Main Aim

Help Jen connect right-hand patterns, last week's jam, a clearer fretboard map, and one small original riff.

Plain version:

`We are turning the right hand into the rhythm engine, then using the A minor pentatonic as a small musical vocabulary.`

## Do Not Overteach

Avoid:

- full scale theory
- all notes on all strings
- modes
- CAGED
- chord naming theory
- every pentatonic position
- all right-hand patterns at once

Teach only:

- one or two right-hand patterns
- E and A strings as root-note highways
- A minor pentatonic as five useful notes
- riff as a repeatable musical idea
- call and response as conversation

## Your 15-Minute Prep Before The Lesson

### 1. Say This Out Loud

"Last week we used the pentatonic shape to have a musical conversation. You asked for right-hand patterns, so today we are going to let the right hand become the rhythm engine, then use that rhythm to shape a small riff."

### 2. Memorise These Natural Notes

Low E string:

```text
Fret: 0  1  3  5  7  8  10 12
Note: E  F  G  A  B  C  D  E
```

A string:

```text
Fret: 0  2  3  5  7  8  10 12
Note: A  B  C  D  E  F  G  A
```

Say:

"The 12th fret is the octave. The note has travelled all the way around and become itself again."

### 3. Know The A Minor Pentatonic Shape

```text
e|----------------5-8-|
B|------------5-8-----|
G|--------5-7---------|
D|----5-7-------------|
A|-5-7----------------|
E|-5-8----------------|
```

Root notes, the home notes, are A:

```text
e|----------------5(A)-8-|
B|-------------5-----8---|
G|---------5-----7-------|
D|-----5-----7(A)--------|
A|-5-----7---------------|
E|-5(A)-8----------------|
```

Say:

"Pentatonic means five notes. In A minor pentatonic, the five notes are A, C, D, E, and G. For now, treat them like five words that already sound good together."

### 4. Prepare Right-Hand Pattern Options

Ask at the start:

"When you say right-hand patterns, do you mean fingerpicking patterns or strumming patterns?"

If she means fingerpicking:

```text
p = thumb
i = index
m = middle
a = ring
```

Pattern 1:

```text
p i m i
```

Pattern 2:

```text
p i m a
```

Pattern 3:

```text
p i m a m i
```

If she means strumming:

```text
D = down
U = up
```

Pattern 1:

```text
D D D D
```

Pattern 2:

```text
D DU D DU
```

Pattern 3:

```text
D D U U D U
```

Use muted strings or open strings if the chord hand gets in the way.

## Lesson Plan

### 1. Arrival And Body Scan

Time: 5 minutes

Ask:

- "What did you notice in your hands this week?"
- "Did you find any E or A string notes?"
- "Did you try writing a riff?"

Do:

- one breath
- shoulders loose
- jaw loose
- left hand shake out
- right hand open-string sound

Say:

"Before we play faster or learn more, we check if the body is available."

Win:

Jen names one body sensation.

### 2. Right-Hand Pattern Choice

Time: 10-15 minutes

Ask:

"When you asked for right-hand patterns, did you mean fingerpicking or strumming?"

Let her answer guide the lesson.

If she says fingerpicking:

Teach:

- thumb is `p`
- index is `i`
- middle is `m`
- ring is `a`
- the hand is learning a small rhythm sentence

Pattern 1:

```text
p i m i
```

Use open strings:

```text
p = A string
i = G string
m = B string
i = G string
```

Pattern 2:

```text
p i m a
```

Use open strings:

```text
p = A string
i = G string
m = B string
a = high e string
```

If she says strumming:

Teach:

```text
D = down
U = up
```

Pattern 1:

```text
D D D D
```

Pattern 2:

```text
D DU D DU
```

Pattern 3:

```text
D D U U D U
```

Use muted strings if chord shapes are distracting.

Say:

"A right-hand pattern is rhythm you can hold. It gives the left hand somewhere to live."

Win:

Jen can repeat one pattern four times without stopping.

### 3. Rest Stroke And Free Stroke Recap

Time: 5 minutes

Use the open high E or B string.

Rest stroke:

- pluck the string
- finger lands/rests on the next string
- sound is fuller and more supported

Free stroke:

- pluck the string
- finger moves through the air
- sound is lighter and more floating

Say:

"Neither is better. They are two colours."

Tiny exercise:

```text
Rest stroke: 4 slow notes
Free stroke: 4 slow notes
Jen describes the difference in sound.
```

Win:

Jen can hear or feel one difference.

### 4. E And A Strings As Root-Note Highways

Time: 8-10 minutes

Say:

"The low E and A strings are like highways. Guitarists use them to find home notes for chords, riffs, and scale shapes."

Teach:

- open string is fret 0
- each fret is one step
- fret 12 is the octave
- dot markers are landmarks
- do not worry about every sharp and flat yet

Low E string natural notes:

```text
0 E | 1 F | 3 G | 5 A | 7 B | 8 C | 10 D | 12 E
```

A string natural notes:

```text
0 A | 2 B | 3 C | 5 D | 7 E | 8 F | 10 G | 12 A
```

Tiny tasks:

1. Find A on low E string.
2. Find C on low E string.
3. Find E on A string.
4. Find D on A string.

Say before each one:

"Name it, then play it."

Win:

Jen can find two notes slowly without random searching.

### 5. Pentatonic As Vocabulary

Time: 10 minutes

Say:

"Last week we were already speaking. Today we are naming the little vocabulary we used."

Teach:

- penta = five
- tonic = notes/tones
- A minor pentatonic = five notes that work around A
- notes: A, C, D, E, G

Do not teach:

- mode names
- minor scale formula
- every pentatonic box

Shape:

```text
e|----------------5-8-|
B|------------5-8-----|
G|--------5-7---------|
D|----5-7-------------|
A|-5-7----------------|
E|-5-8----------------|
```

Tiny tasks:

1. Play the shape slowly upward.
2. Stop on an A root.
3. Play only three notes.
4. Repeat those three notes with rhythm.

Say:

"The scale is not the music yet. The music starts when you choose, repeat, pause, and answer."

Win:

Jen makes a tiny phrase instead of only running the shape.

### 6. Pattern Into Riff

Time: 15 minutes

Ask:

"Show me whatever riff you wrote, even if it is tiny or unfinished."

If she has a riff:

- listen first
- find the strongest part
- repeat it twice
- change the ending
- name it as her riff

If she does not have a riff:

Build this together.

Option A:

```text
e|----------------|
B|----------------|
G|------5-7-5-----|
D|--7-7-------7---|
A|----------------|
E|----------------|
```

Option B:

```text
e|----------------|
B|----------------|
G|--5-7-5---------|
D|--------7-5-7---|
A|----------------|
E|----------------|
```

Option C:

```text
e|----------------|
B|-----5-8-5------|
G|-7-7-------7----|
D|----------------|
A|----------------|
E|----------------|
```

Say:

"A riff is a small idea that wants to come back."

Riff recipe:

1. Choose three notes.
2. Choose one right-hand pattern or rhythm.
3. Repeat it.
4. Change the last note.

Connect to her request:

"The right-hand pattern is what makes the riff feel alive. The left hand chooses notes; the right hand gives them pulse."

Win:

Jen leaves with one repeatable riff fragment.

### 7. Call And Response

Time: 5-10 minutes

Use the riff as the question.

Ayla answers with a tiny pentatonic phrase.

Then swap:

- Ayla asks
- Jen answers

Rules:

- no more than three or four notes
- pause between phrases
- rhythm matters more than speed

Say:

"Conversation needs space. If you never pause, nobody can answer."

Win:

Jen plays one question phrase and one answer phrase.

### 8. Optional C Chord Gap Check

Time: 5 minutes only if calm

Say:

"This showed up as a gap before, so we are just touching it. We are not trying to master it today."

C chord:

```text
e|--0--|
B|--1--|
G|--0--|
D|--2--|
A|--3--|
E|--x--|
```

Ask:

- Which finger feels awkward?
- Which string is hardest to keep clean?

Win:

Jen identifies the difficulty.

Skip this if she is tired or the riff work is alive.

### 9. Reflection And Home Task

Time: 5 minutes

Ask:

- "What sounded most like music today?"
- "What felt foggy?"
- "What do you want to keep alive this week?"

Home task:

1. Keep one right-hand pattern alive.
2. Find natural notes on E and A strings up to fret 12.
3. Keep one pentatonic riff alive.
4. Play call and response with yourself: one question, one answer.

Optional:

Record the riff on a phone, even badly.

## If The Lesson Starts To Feel Too Much

Step down.

Use this order:

1. Body scan.
2. One open-string sound.
3. One right-hand pattern.
4. One root note.
5. Three pentatonic notes.
6. One repeated rhythm.

Say:

"We do not need more material. We need clearer contact with one thing."
