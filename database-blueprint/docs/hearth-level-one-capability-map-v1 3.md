# Hearth Level 1 Capability Map V1

Date: 2026-07-18
Status: Current curriculum direction; prototype thresholds require teacher review

## Decision

The Hearth Mastery's Journey is now owned by Hearth learning outcomes.

QJam remains an important technical roadmap and source collection, but it is
not the curriculum spine. Trinity, RSL, ABRSM, JustinGuitar, the knowledge
library, teaching experience, and real learner evidence are also inputs. No
external source decides the Journey by itself.

This change does not throw away the QJam work. It changes its role:

- before: QJam level and topic completion defined the route;
- now: Hearth capabilities define the route;
- QJam items are mapped underneath those capabilities as technical resources,
  lesson candidates, and progression evidence.

## Plain Level 1 Promise

By the end of Level 1, the learner can prepare to play, keep a simple pulse,
use a small open-chord vocabulary, navigate and phrase with a minor pentatonic
map, and take part in a short piece of music as rhythm player, lead player, or
both. The learner can hear and describe basic musical choices, practise the
weakest part over several days, and identify the next useful step.

Level 1 means **usable and musical**, not perfect.

## Starting Assumption

Foundation has already introduced the learner to the simulator, the guitar,
basic listening, physical contact, confusion recovery, and music as language.

Level 1 does not assume a total beginner, but it does expect gaps. The entry
check finds the real starting point and can recommend a recovery activity
without treating the learner as a failure.

## The Seven Visible Capability Families

These are the learner-friendly families that should shape Level 1. More
technical tags may remain in the data, but the learner should not need to
manage a wall of curriculum categories.

1. **Keep time** - pulse, rhythm, strumming, and groove.
2. **Make harmony** - chords and changes that continue in time.
3. **Navigate notes** - fretboard maps, scales, clean movement, and technique.
4. **Listen and understand** - ear, TAB, diagrams, theory, and musical words.
5. **Make music** - songs, jams, phrases, accompaniment, and role exchange.
6. **Create and express** - variation, improvisation, riffs, and personal
   choices.
7. **Learn sustainably** - tuning, body awareness, practice, feedback,
   consolidation, and reflection.

## Working Level 1 Capabilities

The evidence stages are defined in
`external-music-exam-benchmark-plan-v1.md`: contact, attempted, demonstrated,
applied musically, consolidated, and externally assessed.

These thresholds are internal prototype standards, not exam equivalence.

### A. Keep time

#### `L1-TIME-01` Hold a simple pulse

- Outcome: maintain a steady quarter-note pulse for at least eight bars at a
  comfortable chosen tempo.
- Evidence: tapping, muted strings, one note, or one chord recorded as
  `demonstrated`.
- Stronger evidence: repeat it on another day or inside a song/groove.
- Primary nodes: Practice, Play, Do.

#### `L1-TIME-02` Use a basic rhythm pattern musically

- Outcome: play at least one simple strum or picking pattern without losing
  the main pulse.
- Evidence: apply the pattern to a chord, groove, or backing track.
- Primary nodes: Do, Practice, Play.

### B. Make harmony

#### `L1-HARM-01` Build a working open-chord vocabulary

- Outcome: form at least four usable common open chords, including major and
  minor colour.
- Breadth target: encounter the common eight-chord set without requiring all
  eight to be consolidated at once.
- Evidence: each chord is labelled usable, shaky, or missing from actual sound
  and hand evidence rather than memory alone.
- Primary nodes: Do, Practice, Know.

#### `L1-HARM-02` Change chords without abandoning time

- Outcome: repeat at least two useful chord pairs for eight cycles at a
  comfortable tempo.
- Evidence: a chord loop, song section, or teacher-observed take.
- Primary nodes: Do, Practice, Play.

### C. Navigate notes

#### `L1-MAP-01` Find home inside a minor pentatonic map

- Outcome: navigate the first minor-pentatonic position, with A minor as the
  default learning key, and locate at least two root/home notes.
- Easier path: work in two- or three-string regions with a root as the safety
  point before joining the whole shape.
- Evidence: find the region, play it slowly, and return to a root without a
  diagram at least once.
- Primary nodes: Know, Study, Do, Practice.

#### `L1-MAP-02` Turn the map into a phrase

- Outcome: make a short two- to four-note phrase with timing, repetition, and
  space rather than only playing the scale up and down.
- Evidence: call and response, a tiny solo, or a recorded phrase.
- Primary nodes: Do, Play, Create.

### D. Listen and understand

#### `L1-EAR-01` Hear pulse and home

- Outcome: recognise whether playing stayed with the pulse and experience a
  chosen root as a musical home or landing point.
- Evidence: a listening choice followed by playing or a plain-language
  explanation.
- Primary nodes: Hearth, Study, Play.

#### `L1-READ-01` Follow a simple guitar representation

- Outcome: follow a basic chord diagram, fretboard diagram, TAB fragment, and
  simple rhythm grid with support.
- Evidence: translate one visual example into sound on the guitar.
- Required stage: `attempted`; fluent staff reading is not a Level 1 gate.
- Primary nodes: Study, Know, Do.

#### `L1-KNOW-01` Explain the current musical relationship

- Outcome: say in plain language that chords provide a harmonic ground and
  scale notes can become phrases over or around that ground.
- Evidence: explain it, demonstrate it, or answer a contextual question.
- Primary nodes: Know, Study, Play.

### E. Make music

#### `L1-PLAY-01` Join a musical exchange

- Outcome: participate in a call and response, jam, backing-track exercise, or
  rhythm-and-lead exchange without needing many notes.
- Evidence: at least one complete exchange with a clear role.
- Primary nodes: Play, Journey.

#### `L1-SONG-01` Carry one complete small piece

- Outcome: perform one complete beginner-appropriate song, song section, or
  deliberately arranged mini-piece using Level 1 material.
- Evidence: live teacher observation, recording, or complete guided take.
- The piece may foreground chords, pentatonic material, or both.
- Primary nodes: Play, Practice, Mastery.

#### `L1-ROLE-01` Experience rhythm and lead as related roles

- Outcome: try both holding the musical ground and speaking with a phrase.
- Evidence: swap roles with a person, loop, or backing track.
- Required stage: `attempted`; both roles do not need equal fluency.
- Primary nodes: Play, Journey.

### F. Create and express

#### `L1-CREATE-01` Make and keep one musical choice

- Outcome: create or vary one riff, phrase, rhythm, call and response, or tiny
  arrangement.
- Evidence: save a recording, short note, TAB fragment, or structured project
  memory that allows the learner to return to it.
- Primary nodes: Create, Play.

#### `L1-STYLE-01` Meet one style without making it compulsory

- Outcome: experience how a groove or phrasing choice changes musical feel.
- Evidence: a blues, rock, folk, flamenco, or other sourced Level 1 encounter.
- Blues is a strong default route, not the definition of Level 1.
- Primary nodes: Play, Study, Mastery.

### G. Learn sustainably

#### `L1-PREP-01` Prepare the instrument and body

- Outcome: tune with a reference or tool, check shoulders/jaw/hands/breath,
  and produce attentive open-string sound.
- Evidence: repeated lesson opening or practice-session record.
- Primary nodes: Foundation, Hearth, Practice.

#### `L1-PRACTICE-01` Repeat a real weakness over time

- Outcome: use a 15- to 20-minute practice plan across at least three separate
  days, focusing on an observed gap rather than random activity.
- Evidence: practice records plus one comparison note or take.
- Primary nodes: Practice, Journey.

#### `L1-REFLECT-01` Name evidence and choose the next gradient

- Outcome: identify what felt musical, what broke first, what needs repetition,
  and what should happen next.
- Evidence: learner reflection, teacher note, or end-of-lesson review.
- Primary nodes: Hearth, Journey.

## Level Completion Rule

Level 1 should not unlock because eight lesson screens were opened.

Working completion requires:

- all seven capability families have been encountered;
- `L1-TIME-01`, `L1-HARM-02`, `L1-MAP-02`, `L1-PLAY-01`, and
  `L1-PRACTICE-01` have at least `demonstrated` evidence;
- `L1-SONG-01` has one complete guided or independent take;
- `L1-READ-01` and `L1-ROLE-01` have at least `attempted` evidence;
- the learner has one saved creative choice;
- the learner or teacher has recorded a next-step reflection.

A learner may continue exploring Level 2 while strengthening a Level 1 gap,
but the system must show the gap honestly rather than manufacture completion.

## Hour-Lesson Rhythm

The current six-block lesson shape is useful internally, but the learner-facing
hour should honour the fuller design brief:

1. **Tune in** - tuner, body, attention, and open sound.
2. **Recall** - bring back last evidence and the current musical purpose.
3. **Warm up** - prepare the hands and brain with relevant material.
4. **Learn** - one new idea in plain language with useful media.
5. **Try** - first supported attempt and an easier path if needed.
6. **Practise** - focused repetition using the appropriate node tool.
7. **Play** - groove, song, jam, backing track, or role exchange.
8. **Create** - make one choice, variation, phrase, or arrangement.
9. **Encounter mastery** - briefly see or hear the skill developed in real
   artistry when a suitable sourced example exists.
10. **Reflect and prepare** - review evidence, save feedback, and set the next
    practice step.

These are learning functions, not ten compulsory full-screen lectures. A short
session may combine or compress them. Journey should launch node-owned tools
rather than rebuilding those tools inside the lesson.

## Comparison With The Existing Eight Lessons

### Current Lesson 1: Level 1 Entry Check

What works:

- body, pulse, clean sound, known chord, and next-gradient logic;
- honest diagnosis instead of pretending every learner starts equally.

What changes:

- make this a **Level 1 preflight**, not one of the eight curriculum lessons;
- store its findings as learner evidence and use them to select recovery or
  consolidation activities;
- do not mark capabilities complete from the check alone.

### Current Lesson 2: The 8 Open Chords

What works:

- creates a useful inventory of known, shaky, and missing chords;
- avoids pretending every chord is already learned.

What changes:

- separate breadth from readiness: encounter eight, build a working set of at
  least four, and consolidate useful pairs over time;
- begin one real song or accompaniment immediately;
- do not make eight clean chords in one session the implied success condition.

### Current Lesson 3: Common-Finger Chord Changes

What works:

- small physical gradient;
- strong timing and musical-loop connection.

What changes:

- include one right-hand pattern rather than treating chord changes as only a
  fretting-hand problem;
- connect the chosen change to the Level 1 song.

### Current Lesson 4: Pentatonic Shape 1

What works:

- introduces a reusable fretboard map;
- already moves from shape to a two-note phrase.

What changes:

- teach root/home notes before requiring the complete box;
- offer two- or three-string regions as safety zones;
- add listening and a simple diagram/TAB translation;
- keep A minor as the default route, not the only possible key forever.

### Current Lesson 5: Pentatonic Phrasing

What works:

- one of the strongest existing lessons;
- turns a scale into speech through rhythm, space, and call and response.

What changes:

- save one phrase as creative evidence;
- compare an early and later take;
- connect phrasing to the selected song or groove where possible.

### Current Lesson 6: First Blues Solo Entry

What works:

- good limitation of choices;
- brings technique into musical feeling.

What changes:

- treat blues as the default style path, not a universal Level 1 requirement;
- allow another sourced groove/style to prove the same capability;
- include a brief mastery example and listening prompt.

### Current Lesson 7: Chords Meet Pentatonics

What works:

- this is a core Hearth lesson because it connects previously separate skills;
- rhythm and lead become related musical roles.

What changes:

- make role exchange explicit;
- use the learner's Level 1 song or arrangement rather than only an abstract
  two-bar loop;
- save the arrangement or performance evidence.

### Current Lesson 8: QJam Level 1 Integration

What works:

- checks pulse, chords, pentatonic orientation, a phrase, and reflection;
- values usable musical evidence over theoretical perfection.

What changes:

- rename it **Level 1 Integration**;
- use capability evidence rather than QJam topic completion;
- include a complete song or mini-piece, practice history, reading/TAB contact,
  role exchange, and one creative choice;
- recommend Level 2, consolidation, or a recovery quest without shame.

## Main Gaps In The Current Route

1. A complete song or piece is not yet a strong Level 1 requirement.
2. Listening and aural development are present in spirit but not systematic.
3. TAB, diagrams, and rhythm reading are under-evidenced.
4. Right-hand development is weaker than chord and pentatonic content.
5. Practice across multiple days is not part of the completion rule.
6. Creative work is prompted but not reliably saved as evidence.
7. Blues is carrying too much of the style identity.
8. Mastery encounters are not consistently connected to the lesson skill.
9. The final review does not yet assemble evidence from every relevant node.

## Recommended Level 1 Route

### Preflight: Find the real starting point

Diagnostic only. Capture body, pulse, tone, known chords, fretboard contact,
learner interests, and current gaps.

### Lesson 1: Pulse, sound, and one groove

Establish the practice rhythm and make the first repeated action musical.

### Lesson 2: A working chord set and a song doorway

Inventory the common chords, choose the current working set, and begin a real
song or accompaniment.

### Lesson 3: Chord changes and the right hand

Keep time through one or two changes using a useful strum or picking pattern.

### Lesson 4: Pentatonic roots, regions, and the map

Hear and find home, learn manageable regions, then connect the full first map.

### Lesson 5: Phrase, listen, and answer

Use few notes, rhythm, repetition, and space; save one phrase.

### Lesson 6: Groove and improvisation

Use a blues or learner-chosen style route to improvise over a living pulse.

### Lesson 7: Rhythm and lead inside one piece

Learn or arrange a song that allows rhythm and lead roles, then exchange them.

### Lesson 8: Level 1 musical integration

Complete a small performance, review evidence from across the nodes, identify
remaining gaps, and prepare the next practice or Level 2 route.
