# Jen Practice Sheet - Love Itself

Date: 2026-07-22

Practice commitment: 20 minutes a day

Main goal: make one small section of "Love Itself" feel steady and musical,
while continuing the scale and finger work that is already improving.

## A Note For Jen

You are making progress on several fronts at once. The scale patterns are
becoming more musical, the fingers are gaining independence, and you have begun
using rhythm and lead as two parts of the same song.

The whole neck does not need to make sense yet. Learn one area well, notice
where its home notes are, and let the larger map grow from there.

## Daily 20-Minute Practice

### 1. Arrive - 2 minutes

- Tune the guitar.
- Relax the shoulders, jaw, wrists, and thumbs.
- Play a few open strings and listen for a clear sound.

Reminder: calm and clean is more useful than fast.

### 2. Marcello-Style Scale Warm-Up - 4 minutes

- Play the familiar pattern slowly.
- Keep the pulse even.
- Listen to every note.
- Pause and reset if the hand becomes tense.

Goal: one controlled repetition is better than several rushed repetitions.

### 3. Finger Independence - 3 minutes

- Use the finger-independence exercise learned in the lesson.
- Move one finger at a time.
- Keep the other fingers as quiet and relaxed as possible.
- Stop before the hand becomes strained or frustrated.

Goal: control, not speed.

### 4. Love Itself - Rhythm Part - 6 minutes

- Use the open-position version from the lesson.
- Practise only two chord shapes or one small song section at a time.
- Place the harder barre-shaped chord quietly before strumming it.
- Loop the difficult change slowly.
- Keep the rhythm moving, even if the chord is not yet perfect.

Goal: play one short section without stopping.

### 5. F-Position Major-Scale Area - 3 minutes

- Repeat the major-scale pattern learned around the F position.
- Say or notice where the home/root note is.
- Use only a small part of the pattern if the full shape becomes blurry.
- Turn two or three notes into a tiny musical answer.

Goal: treat the pattern as a musical place, not only a finger exercise.

### 6. Rhythm + Lead Conversation - 2 minutes

- Play or record a slow piece of the rhythm part.
- Answer it with a very short scale phrase.
- Leave space between phrases.
- When practising together, swap rhythm and lead roles.

Goal: listen and respond rather than filling every moment with notes.

## If There Is More Time

- Repeat the A minor pentatonic call-and-response work.
- Compare how the A minor pentatonic and the new major-scale area feel.
- Find one root note in each familiar scale area.
- Practise one difficult song chord change three clean times.

## This Week's Useful Wins

- One short section of "Love Itself" played in steady time.
- One difficult chord change that feels less tense.
- One clean Marcello warm-up repetition.
- One tiny phrase from the F-position major-scale area.
- One rhythm-and-lead exchange where both players keep listening.

## After Practice

Choose one question:

- Which chord change became easier?
- Which scale phrase sounded musical?
- Where did tension appear?
- Could I keep the pulse while changing chords?
- What should we repeat in the next lesson?
