## Current Strengths

### 1. Teaching and practice philosophy

The library already supports:

- deliberate and observable practice;
- tension and clean movement;
- learning gradients;
- repetition and retention;
- daily practice structure;
- technique returning to musical use.

Relevant processed sources include Ericsson, Guitar Aerobics, Guitar Exercises
For Dummies, Jamie Andreas, and Aaron Shearer structural notes.

### 2. Music, language, and the brain

Levitin and Patel notes support:

- music as language;
- listening before labels;
- pattern recognition;
- expectation and meaning;
- careful Hearth and Study explanations.

These sources are strong design anchors but require restrained learner-facing
claims.

### 3. Theory and reading structure

The Everything Music Theory source notes provide a useful progression through:

- notation basics;
- rhythm and meter;
- intervals;
- scales and keys;
- chords and harmony;
- transposition.

This is useful for Study. It still needs to become small, guitar-relevant,
interactive learning units.

### 4. Technical exercise material

Technique is the most represented source-note discipline, with 24 notes. This
supports Do and Practice but should not allow the simulator to become
technique-heavy at the expense of listening, songs, groove, and creativity.

## Highest-Priority Gaps

### 1. Play and cultural context

There are currently no source notes mapped to Play.

The first Mississippi Delta / A minor musical-conversation slice requires:

- reviewed cultural and historical context;
- an appropriate listening source;
- clear claim-status notes;
- audible links between people, place, pulse, phrasing, instrument, and role;
- an activity that does not reduce the tradition to a decorative blues label.

Priority: Critical before Play is treated as content complete.

### 2. Level 1 listening and ear development

Only three existing source notes are classified as Ear Training, plus one
Listening note. The simulator's stated philosophy gives listening a much larger
role.

Level 1 needs source-backed material for:

- hearing steady pulse;
- comparing clear and buzzing notes;
- hearing root as home;
- high and low pitch;
- rest versus free stroke tone;
- rhythmic and melodic call and response;
- recognising simple musical change.

Priority: High.

### 3. Songs and complete musical application

The source-note inventory does not yet form a usable Level 1 song pathway.

We need a rights-aware list of songs or original substitutes that can support:

- open chords;
- Am to C movement;
- steady down-up strumming;
- A minor pentatonic rhythm and lead roles;
- one rhythm guitarist and one lead guitarist;
- Jen's request to learn a song using those skills.

Priority: High.

### 4. Mastery encounters

Only four source notes are mapped to Mastery. The node needs sourced, short
encounters with artists, performances, techniques, and musical choices, not a
large unsourced gallery.

Each encounter needs:

- a legal listening or viewing source;
- the exact choice to notice;
- a small experiment;
- a transformation prompt;
- an optional direction to save.

Priority: High after the active Mastery vertical slice is stable.

### 5. Create source connections

Only one source note is mapped directly to Create.

Create does not need a conventional curriculum library, but it does need a
reviewed bank of:

- compositional constraints;
- lyric, riff, rhythm, melody, chord, structure, and mood techniques;
- examples of transformation rather than imitation;
- ways to continue or revisit a seed;
- safe cross-node handoffs from learned material.

Priority: Medium. Existing learner-generated material remains the main input.

### 6. Know reference depth

Know has only six mapped source notes despite owning the bookshelf and reference
library. The catalogue is large, but most books are not yet converted into
small navigable reference entries.

Priority: Medium. Build entries from active Level 1 questions rather than
trying to process the full library.

### 7. QJam verification

All 86 QJam video records still require review.

Each promoted video needs:

- current link check;
- correct title and creator;
- exact useful timestamp or section;
- direct roadmap purpose;
- level and prerequisite check;
- access status;
- fallback if unavailable.

Priority: Review the four Level 1 records first. Do not begin with the full 86.

### 8. Official assessment benchmarks

Official guitar assessment systems are not yet represented as structured
sources or source notes.

They should be catalogued separately from ordinary teaching resources because
their versions, requirements, repertoire rights, and assessment claims need
special handling.

Priority: Planning now; detailed mapping after current nodes stabilise.

## Level 1 Source Work Order

Build a compact, balanced Level 1 evidence pack before expanding later levels.

### Batch 1: Physical foundations and clean sound

- fretting contact;
- picking and fingerstroke basics;
- tension and posture;
- six-string orientation;
- clean-tone listening.

Use existing processed technique sources first.

### Batch 2: Pulse, subdivision, and groove

- steady pulse;
- quarter and eighth-note movement;
- down-up strumming;
- accents;
- first blues or shuffle feel;
- cultural context for groove.

This batch needs additional listening sources and Play context.

### Batch 3: Chords and song use

- open-chord audit;
- A minor and C;
- economical chord changes;
- chord checking;
- one complete song or rights-safe substitute.

### Batch 4: A minor pentatonic as musical language

- shape and roots;
- three small safety boxes;
- root notes in time;
- phrasing and space;
- call and response;
- rhythm and lead roles;
- improvisation and first personal riff.

This batch directly supports Ayla and Jen.

### Batch 5: Musical literacy and ear

- musical alphabet;
- note landmarks;
- interval language;
- tablature and simple rhythm reading;
- hearing root, pulse, tone, and phrase response.

### Batch 6: Reflection, creativity, and mastery

- body and attention reflection;
- what repetition changes;
- one saved creative seed;
- one reviewed artist encounter;
- one end-of-level musical demonstration.

## Credit-Efficient Processing Strategy

1. Use existing 97 source notes before extracting more.
2. Review only the four Level 1 QJam videos first.
3. Select one or two sources for each uncovered Level 1 gap.
4. Extract table of contents or one chapter before deep-processing a book.
5. OCR only pages required for the current batch.
6. Promote source notes into original content only after the node activity is
   clear.
7. Record rejected or unsuitable sources so the work is not repeated.

This prevents the content library from consuming credits without producing a
usable learning path.
