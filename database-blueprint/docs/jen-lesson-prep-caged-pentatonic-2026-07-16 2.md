# Jen Lesson Prep - CAGED And A Minor Pentatonic - 2026-07-16

## Context

Ayla is preparing for Jen's lesson today.

Jen's current path is still consolidation:

- right-hand patterns
- A minor pentatonic
- root notes as safety points
- musical conversation
- clean sound
- making drills become music

Jen does not need a big new theory dump.

## Teaching Decision

CAGED can be introduced today, but only as a tiny map glimpse.

Do not teach the full CAGED system yet.

Use CAGED to answer one simple question:

`Why do chord shapes, root notes, and pentatonic boxes belong together?`

## Plain Explanation For Jen

CAGED is a way of seeing the guitar neck through five familiar chord shapes:

- C
- A
- G
- E
- D

For today, the important idea is not memorizing all five shapes.

The important idea is:

`Chord shapes are landmarks. Pentatonic notes live around those landmarks.`

## Safe Lesson Aim

Connect A minor pentatonic to one nearby chord shape without overloading the lesson.

Suggested micro-focus:

`A minor pentatonic box 1 lives around an A minor / E-shape area. The root note A is home.`

If that feels too abstract, simplify further:

`Find A. Play a tiny pentatonic phrase. Come back to A.`

## Lesson Shape

### 1. Tune In

Time: 5 minutes

- body scan
- tune guitar
- open-string sound
- relaxed shoulders, jaw, hands

Win condition:

Jen arrives in her body before playing.

### 2. Review The Safe Map

Time: 10 minutes

- find A root notes slowly
- revisit the A minor pentatonic shape
- stop on A and let it sound settled

Win condition:

Jen can hear/feel A as home.

### 3. Right-Hand Doorway

Time: 10 minutes

Use the thing Jen asked for:

- one right-hand pattern
- one pulse
- repeat four times
- stay calm and even

Win condition:

The pattern feels like music, not a test.

### 4. Tiny CAGED Glimpse

Time: 5-8 minutes

Say:

`This is not a new mountain. It is just a map clue. Guitar players use chord shapes as landmarks. The pentatonic scale is not floating randomly. It sits around chord homes.`

Show only one connection:

- A as home/root
- the nearby A minor feeling/chord area
- pentatonic notes around it

Avoid:

- all five CAGED shapes
- memorizing the system
- transposing
- full-neck explanation

Win condition:

Jen understands that scales and chords are connected, even if she cannot explain CAGED yet.

### 5. Make It Music

Time: 15-20 minutes

Use A minor pentatonic:

- play only roots with rhythm
- add one nearby note
- add one more nearby note
- make a tiny phrase
- repeat it
- answer it differently

Use metronome or pulse:

- 60 BPM as the main safe tempo
- 76 BPM only if calm
- 100 BPM only as a light feel-test, not a challenge

Win condition:

Jen makes a small repeatable phrase.

### 6. Musical Conversation

Time: 10 minutes

- one person holds A minor or a simple pulse
- the other plays tiny pentatonic phrases
- leave space
- swap roles

Win condition:

Jen experiences the pentatonic as conversation.

### 7. Reflection

Time: 5 minutes

Ask:

- What felt like home?
- What sounded most musical?
- What felt confusing?
- Did the chord-shape map clue help or feel like too much?
- What should we repeat next time?

## Teacher Reminder

If Jen looks overloaded, drop CAGED immediately.

Return to:

- A is home
- small phrase
- steady pulse
- call and response

The win is not that Jen learns CAGED today.

The win is that the pentatonic scale feels less random.
