# Jen Lesson Review - Love Itself, Rhythm + Lead

Date: 2026-07-22

## What Happened

- Reviewed the Marcello-style scale warm-up.
- Used the scale pattern in musical call and response.
- Repeated the finger-independence exercises.
- Began learning Leonard Cohen's "Love Itself."
- Transposed the capo arrangement so it could be played in an open position.
- Began working with harder barre-shaped chords from the new arrangement.
- Learned a major-scale pattern around the F position.
- Began playing together in two roles: one person on rhythm and one on lead.

## Honest Progress Note

Progress is being made across the warm-up, finger independence, scale work,
rhythm playing, and musical response. Rhythm and lead are beginning to work as
parts of the same piece rather than as separate exercises.

Nothing here should be marked mastered yet. The chord shapes, scale patterns,
clean changes, timing, and role exchange all need repetition.

## What Helped

- Call and response made the scale pattern musical.
- Using a real song gave the chord and scale work a clear purpose.
- Separating rhythm and lead made it possible to start playing together.
- Transposing the arrangement allowed the song to be explored without relying
  on the original capo position.

## Current Difficulty

The whole guitar neck still feels unclear. This is expected at this stage. The
next safe step is not to memorise the entire neck at once. Build one reliable
position, notice its root notes, and gradually connect it to the nearby shapes.

## Next Safe Lesson Step

Continue "Love Itself" in small sections. Strengthen the rhythm part first,
then use the F-position major-scale area for a short lead response. Swap roles
only when both parts can continue slowly without stopping.

## Journey Evidence Note

This lesson provides evidence of contact and continued practice in:

- physical preparation and finger independence
- scale-map recognition
- musical phrasing and call and response
- chord and rhythm coordination
- song learning
- rhythm/lead role exchange

It does not yet provide evidence of mastery or readiness to leave Level 1
consolidation.
