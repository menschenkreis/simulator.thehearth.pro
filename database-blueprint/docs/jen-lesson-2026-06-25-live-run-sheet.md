# Jen Lesson Live Run-Sheet

Date: 2026-06-25

## Main Thread

Right hand first.

Then root notes.

Then pentatonic riff.

Then call and response.

Plain sentence:

`The right hand gives the music a pulse. The left hand chooses words. Together they make a sentence.`

## Do Not Teach Today

- modes
- all pentatonic positions
- CAGED
- full chord theory
- every note on the neck
- notation theory
- too many right-hand patterns

## Opening

Time: 5 minutes

Ask:

- "How did your hands feel this week?"
- "Did you find any E or A string notes?"
- "Did you write or find a little riff?"
- "When you asked for right-hand patterns, did you mean fingerpicking or strumming?"

Do:

- one breath
- shoulders loose
- jaw loose
- hands shake out
- one open-string sound

Say:

`Before we add information, we check if the body is ready.`

## Choose One Right-Hand Track

Time: 15 minutes

Choose only one track unless she clearly wants both.

### If Fingerpicking

Explain:

```text
p = thumb
i = index
m = middle
a = ring
```

Pattern 1:

```text
p i m i
```

Use:

```text
p = A string
i = G string
m = B string
i = G string
```

Say:

`This is a rhythm sentence. Keep it alive before you make it fancy.`

Optional Pattern 2:

```text
p i m a
```

### If Strumming

Explain:

```text
D = down
U = up
```

Pattern 1:

```text
D D D D
```

Pattern 2:

```text
D DU D DU
```

Say:

`The hand is the drummer. Even before chords, it can hold time.`

## Rest Stroke / Free Stroke Recap

Time: 5 minutes

Rest stroke:

`Play through the string and come to rest on the next string.`

Free stroke:

`Play the string and let the finger move freely without landing on the next string.`

Ask:

`Which one sounds stronger? Which one feels lighter?`

## E And A String Root Notes

Time: 10 minutes

Low E string:

```text
0 E | 1 F | 3 G | 5 A | 7 B | 8 C | 10 D | 12 E
```

A string:

```text
0 A | 2 B | 3 C | 5 D | 7 E | 8 F | 10 G | 12 A
```

Say:

`The E and A strings are highways. They help you find home notes quickly.`

Only ask her to find:

- E
- A
- C
- D

Do not ask for every note.

## A Minor Pentatonic

Time: 10 minutes

Say:

`Pentatonic means five notes. A minor pentatonic gives us A, C, D, E, and G. Treat them like five words that already belong in the same sentence.`

Shape:

```text
e|----------------5-8-|
B|------------5-8-----|
G|--------5-7---------|
D|----5-7-------------|
A|-5-7----------------|
E|-5-8----------------|
```

Home notes:

```text
e|----------------5(A)-8-|
B|-------------5-----8---|
G|---------5-----7-------|
D|-----5-----7(A)--------|
A|-5-----7---------------|
E|-5(A)-8----------------|
```

## Make A Riff

Time: 10 minutes

Recipe:

1. Choose three notes.
2. Choose one right-hand pattern.
3. Repeat it.
4. Change the ending.

Example:

```text
e|----------------|
B|----------------|
G|------5-7-5-----|
D|--7-7-------7---|
A|----------------|
E|----------------|
```

Say:

`A riff is a tiny idea that wants to come back.`

## Call And Response

Time: 10 minutes

Rules:

- Ayla plays a short question.
- Jen answers.
- Leave silence between phrases.
- Keep it in A minor pentatonic.
- Better to be clear than busy.

Say:

`This is the guitar conversation. You do not need many words. You need to answer.`

## Reflection

Time: 5 minutes

Ask:

- "Which right-hand pattern felt most natural?"
- "Which note felt like home?"
- "What confused you?"
- "What would you like to do next week?"

Write down:

- one win
- one gap
- one interest
- one home task

## Home Task

Give only three tasks:

1. Keep one right-hand pattern alive for 3 minutes.
2. Find E and A string notes up to fret 12.
3. Write one tiny A minor pentatonic riff.

Optional:

Record 10 seconds of the riff.

## If It Gets Messy

Use this rescue path:

1. Stop.
2. Ask: "Was there a word or idea that got foggy?"
3. Return to one string.
4. Play one note.
5. Add one rhythm.

Say:

`We are not behind. We just found the next smaller step.`
