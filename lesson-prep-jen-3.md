## 2. The Skipped Gradient — Shape/Block Concept

You taught her a pentatonic shape but didn't explain what a "shape" or "block" actually IS. Here's how to fill that gap:

### What to say:

> "When I showed you that pentatonic pattern last week, I gave you a shape — a pattern of dots on the fretboard. But I didn't explain what a shape actually is.
>
> A shape is a **moveable pattern**. The dots stay in the same relative positions, but you can slide the whole thing up or down the neck to play in different keys.
>
> The pentatonic shape I showed you works in the key of A when you start on fret 5. But if you slide it up so the first note is on fret 7, you're now in B. Fret 8 = C. Fret 3 = G.
>
> This is the power of guitar — learn one shape, play it in 12 keys. The pattern is the same. The starting position changes.
>
> Think of it like a stamp. The stamp is the shape. Where you press it on the paper is the key."

### Demo:
- Play the pentatonic shape starting on fret 5 (A minor)
- Slide it up to fret 7 (B minor) — same shape, different sound
- Slide it to fret 3 (G minor) — same shape again
- Let her hear that the *pattern* sounds the same, just higher/lower

### Check:
- Can she explain it back to you?
- Can she move the shape to a new position on her own?

## 3. New Drill — Flamenco-Style Phrasing

Since she's been playing flamenco, build on that interest. This drill uses the pentatonic shape she already knows but adds **phrasing** — the art of making notes sound like sentences, not just scales.

### The Drill: "Call and Response"

**Concept:** Play a short phrase (call), then answer it with a different phrase (response). This is how flamenco guitarists communicate — it's musical conversation.

**Setup:**
- Use the A minor pentatonic shape (fret 5 position)
- She knows this shape already

**How it works:**

1. **You play a 3-4 note phrase** (the call)
2. **She plays a 3-4 note phrase back** (the response)
3. The response should feel like an "answer" — it can go up when yours went down, or end on a different note

**Example calls:**
- Fret 5 → 7 → 8 (ascending, feels like a question)
- Fret 8 → 7 → 5 (descending, feels like an answer)
- Fret 5 → 7 → 5 (stays in place, feels like a statement)

**Rules for her:**
- Keep it short (3-4 notes max)
- Use notes from the shape she knows
- Don't think too hard — feel it
- There's no wrong answer

**Why this works:**
- It's flamenco-flavoured (call and response is core to flamenco)
- It uses the shape she already knows (no new patterns)
- It teaches phrasing without理论 terminology
- It's fun and interactive
- It builds her ear (she has to listen to your call before responding)

**Progression:**
- Start slow, let her take her time
- Speed up as she gets comfortable
- Add rhythm (try clapping a beat while doing it)
- Eventually, she makes up both the call AND the response

## 6. Quick Reference — Pentatonic Shape (A minor position)

```
e|---5---8---
B|---5---7---
G|---5---7---
D|---5---7---
A|---5---7---
E|---5---8---
```

Notes: A C D E G A (A minor pentatonic)

To transpose: move the whole shape up/down. Same pattern, new key.
