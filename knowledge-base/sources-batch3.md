## 14. Bossa Nova for Guitar — Paul Donat
**Credit:** Paul Donat, "Bossa Nova for Guitar" (©1994)
**Maps to:** Play → Brazilian, Jazz Branch → Bossa Nova

### Techniques
- **Nylon-string guitar** ("violão") — warm, bright tone from flesh + nails
- **No picks** — thumb plays bass (A & E strings), fingers 1-2-3 pluck chord tones (D, G, B)
- **Steady bass on 1 & 3** vs syncopated chords — the essence of bossa nova
- Right-hand independence (thumb vs fingers) = pianist-like separation

### Rhythms taught
- Classic Bossa Nova rhythm (Example 1)
- Samba-adaptable rhythm (Example 18)
- Variations by removing ties from core patterns
- Progressive rhythm exercises (6 steps from simple to master)

### Key figures referenced
- Antonio Carlos Jobim, João Gilberto, Vinicius de Morães, Stan Getz
- Baden Powell, Carlos Lyra, Roberto Menescal, Luiz Bonfa

### Learning approach (maps to our tech)
- Gradient-perfect: simple unsyncopated → alternating bass → syncopation → master rhythm
- Music as language: hear the style first (listen to recordings), then speak it
- Context-driven: teaches the history and culture alongside the mechanics

## 16. Super Chops: Jazz Guitar Technique in 20 Weeks — Howard Roberts
**Credit:** Howard Roberts, "Super Chops: Jazz Guitar Technique in 20 Weeks" (CPP/Belwin)
**Maps to:** Jazz Branch → Technique, Doing → Drills (structured 20-week program)

### Structure
- Equipment setup (guitar, pick, metronome, tape recorder, timer)
- Picking/fingerings fundamentals
- Key centers
- Fingerboard navigation
- Line shaping
- 20-week progressive program with project lessons
- Advanced articulation: hammer-ons, pull-offs, slides

### Method
- 50 minutes/day, 6 days/week
- Accuracy over speed — speed is a byproduct of precision
- Progressive: simple picking → fingerings → articulations → review cycles
- Avoids "flam" (double attacks) through proper pick technique
- Classical discipline applied to jazz

### Maps to learning tech
- Hubbard gradient: each week builds on the last, never too steep
- Clay demo: fingerboard = the map you must be able to draw
- Misunderstood word: equipment setup and picking mechanics defined before music starts

## 17. Principles of Correct Practice for Guitar — Jamie Andreas
**Credit:** Jamie Andreas, "The Principles of Correct Practice for Guitar" (©1999, Seven Eyes Publications)
**Maps to:** Foundation → Practice Mechanics, Doing → Drills, Study → Body Awareness

### Core Principles
1. **Mental Intention Precedes Physical Action** — technique is mental, not just physical
2. **Sympathetic Tension Elimination** — unnecessary tension is the root of most problems
3. **Muscle Memory is Double-Edged** — practice wrong and you learn wrong permanently

### Tools (practice methods)
- **No Tempo Practice** — remove time pressure to discover correct sensations
- **Posing** — freeze at any point to analyse position and tension
- **Rotating Attention** — shift focus between body parts to build total awareness
- **Gradual Pressure Technique** — engage strings slowly to eliminate tension
- **Following** — let the finger follow the sensation, don't force

### Exercises
- The Chair and Pillow (posture foundation)
- The Cup (right hand position)
- String Shifting (right hand navigation, 3 steps)
- Thumb techniques (floating, resting, circling, pushing, bouncing)
- Rest Stroke and Free Stroke (apoyando and tirando)
- Finger Dipping and Arpeggios (exercises 7-14)
- Pickstyle mechanics (holding pick, down/up strokes)

### Maps to learning tech
- **Hubbard's absence of mass**: Andreas says "technique is the ability to control attention to produce specific SENSATIONS" — if you can't feel it, you can't do it
- **Hubbard's misunderstood word**: every physical concept is defined before exercise begins
- **Music as language**: body learns vocabulary before mind tries to make sentences
- **This book = the foundation of HOW to practise, not just what to practise**

## 18. Guitar Building Blocks — Patrick Stefurak
**Credit:** Patrick Stefurak, "Guitar Building Blocks" Second Edition (©1996)
**Maps to:** Foundation, Theory Library, Doing → Drills

### The Six Building Blocks
1. Notes on the neck (ABCDEF and G everywhere)
2. Major chords
3. Minor chords
4. Dominant chords
5. Major scale (3 notes/string, 7 patterns including all modes)
6. Pentatonic scale (2 notes/string, 5 patterns)

### Learning progression (weekly)
- Week 1: Notes on the neck
- Progressive: notes → scales → chords → arpeggios
- "Start with the notes on the neck!"

### Key quote
"Create a practice schedule and stick to it."
"Learn how to practice, and memorize the notes on the neck, and you will have received the most valuable tools offered in this book."

### Maps to learning tech
- Hubbard gradient: 6 blocks, each built from the previous
- The "notes on the neck" = Hubbard's mass — you must know the physical geography before anything else
- Stefurak's "five things" method = Lytton/Pincus classification principle

## 19. Right Hand Development for Jazz Guitar — Renard Hoover
**Credit:** Renard D. Hoover, "Right-Hand Development for Pick-Style Guitar" (©1981, Colgor Publishing)
**Maps to:** Doing → Technique (right hand), Jazz Branch

### Method
- Classical guitar discipline applied to pick-style
- Right hand = where sound is initiated, deserves dedicated study
- 90% of motion from fingers and wrist (not arm)
- Heavy-gauge jazz pick recommended

### Two axioms
1. Pick moves flat across string (no slicing)
2. Shorter stroke distance = better speed and control

### Exercise progression
1. Open-string alternating down/up
2. Tremolo preparation (32nd notes)
3. Cross-string picking (2 adjacent strings, inside/outside)
4. Cross-string + repeated notes
5. Awkward configurations (string skips + repeats)
6. Advanced combinations

### Practice approach
- Begin slowly with metronome
- Accuracy before speed
- Start from beginning even if advanced
- "Allow right hand time to adapt"

### Maps to learning tech
- Hubbard gradient: open strings → adjacent strings → skips → complex
- Clay demo: if you can't do it slow and clean, you can't do it fast
- Isolates ONE variable (right hand) while left hand does nothing
