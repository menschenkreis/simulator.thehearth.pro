# Teaching Methodology — The Hearth Mastery

## The Learning Path

The simulator follows a structured progression through 7 nodes. Each node builds on the last.

```
Foundation → Doing → Knowing → Practice → Play → Create → Mastery
```

**Foundation** gives the *understanding*. **Doing** gives the *skills*. Both are essential.

---

## Foundation (7 Blocks)

The foundation covers everything a beginner needs before they start playing systematically.

| Block | Title | Key Concepts |
|-------|-------|--------------|
| 01 | How to Learn | Three barriers (absence of mass, too steep gradient, misunderstood word), imagination before movement, tension hunt, practice structure (20 min/day) |
| 02 | Learning a Language | Music as language (vocabulary + grammar + conversation), listening before playing, Dictionary as tool |
| 03 | Language of Music | 12 notes, half step = 1 fret, whole step = 2 frets, intervals, root notes |
| 04 | Language of Guitar | 6 strings (EADGBE), frets = half steps, fret 12 = octave, tab reading |
| 05 | The Guitar | Anatomy (headstock, neck, body, bridge, nut), posture, thumb placement, capo |
| 06 | Speaking with Guitar | Rest stroke, free stroke, fretting technique, both hands together |
| 07 | Guitar Conversations | Moving between notes, first exercise (open → fret 2), walking up strings, first chord (E major) |

### Foundation Flow
1. Complete all 7 blocks in order
2. Each block has 4 steps: Understand → Experience → Apply → Own
3. After completing a block, return to the pyramid and click the next one
4. The guide character shows a summary of what was learned, then an intro to the next block

---

## Doing (After Foundation)

The Doing node is where students start actually playing things systematically.

### 6 Categories:

**1. Picking** (start here)
- Alternate picking (down-up-down-up)
- Flatpick basics
- Open strings → single notes → chromatic

**2. Rhythm**
- 16th note subdivisions
- Basic strumming: D DU UDU pattern
- Metronome work

**3. Fretting**
- Chromatic exercise (1-2-3-4 on each string)
- Spider exercise (cross-string patterns)
- Legato (hammer-ons and pull-offs)

**4. Scales**
- Pentatonic minor (moveable shapes)
- Major scale
- Connecting positions across the neck

**5. Arpeggios**
- Major and minor arpeggios (broken chords)
- Open position first

**6. Speed Mechanics**
- Tremolo picking
- Sequences (scale patterns)

## The Shape/Block Concept

A critical concept that must be explained early:

> "A shape is a moveable pattern. The dots stay in the same relative positions, but you can slide the whole thing up or down the neck to play in different keys.
>
> Think of it like a stamp. The stamp is the shape. Where you press it on the paper is the key."

**Demo:** Play the pentatonic shape at fret 5 (A minor), then fret 7 (B minor), then fret 3 (G minor). Same pattern, different sound.

**Common mistake:** Teaching a shape without explaining that it's moveable. This creates a gradient gap — the student knows the pattern but doesn't understand the concept.

## Lesson Structure (45 minutes)

| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 0-5 min | Warm up: chromatic exercise or open strings |
| 5-15 min | Foundation check: run through blocks, find gaps |
| 15-25 min | Fill gradient gaps: explain concepts that were skipped |
| 25-40 min | New drill: build on what they know |
| 40-45 min | Cool down: let them play whatever they want |

## Key Teaching Principles

### 1. Check for the Three Barriers
- **Absence of mass:** Is the physical thing in front of them? (guitar, tab, diagram)
- **Too steep gradient:** Are they jumping ahead too fast? Break it down smaller.
- **Misunderstood word:** Do they understand every term? Stop and clarify.

### 2. Imagination Before Movement
- Have them visualise what their fingers should do before they play
- The clearer the mental picture, the cleaner the physical execution

### 3. Tension Hunt
- Watch for tension: shoulders, jaw, fretting hand
- Do body scans before and during practice
- Tension = the enemy of good technique

### 4. Build on Interest
- If they're drawn to a style (flamenco, blues, rock), lean into it
- Use their motivation as the vehicle for learning
- Let them play what they enjoy

### 5. Listening Before Playing
- Babies listen for months before they speak
- Train the ear first — intervals, scales, chord qualities
- "Can you hear the difference between major and minor?"

## Drill Ideas by Level

### Beginner (Foundation + early Doing)
- **Call and Response:** Play a 3-4 note phrase, they play one back. Uses pentatonic shape. Flamenco-flavoured.
- **Chromatic Walk:** 1-2-3-4 on each string, all 6 strings. Focus on clean notes.
- **Open String Rhythm:** Strum muted strings with D DU UDU pattern. Focus on timing.
- **First Chord Transitions:** E major → A major, slow transitions.

### Intermediate (mid-Doing)
- **Pentatonic Position Shifting:** Move the same shape up and down the neck.
- **Rhythm Variations:** Add syncopation to basic strumming patterns.
- **Arpeggio Patterns:** Break chords into individual notes.
- **Speed Building:** Metronome work — start slow, increase by 5 BPM only when clean.

### Advanced (late Doing + Knowing)
- **Modal Exploration:** Play the same scale pattern starting on different notes.
- **Improvisation Over Backing Tracks:** Apply scales in musical context.
- **Song Transcription:** Learn a song by ear, then check with tab.
- **Composition:** Write a short piece using learned concepts.

## Reference: Pentatonic Shape (A minor position)

```
e|---5---8---
B|---5---7---
G|---5---7---
D|---5---7---
A|---5---7---
E|---5---8---
```

Notes: A C D E G A (A minor pentatonic)

To transpose: move the whole shape up/down. Same pattern, new key.

---

## Reference: E Major Chord

```
e|---0---
B|---0---
G|---1---  (index, fret 1)
D|---2---  (middle, fret 2)
A|---2---  (ring, fret 2)
E|---0---
```

First chord most guitarists learn. All 6 strings. Foundation Block 07.

---

## Reference: Basic Strum Pattern

**D DU UDU**

```
D = Downstroke
U = Upstroke
_ = Rest

D _ D U _ U D U
1 + 2 + 3 + 4 +
```

The most common strumming pattern in popular music. Foundation for all rhythm work.
