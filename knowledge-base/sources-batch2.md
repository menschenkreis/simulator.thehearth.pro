# Knowledge Base — Source Books (Batch 2)

## 5. Jazz Guitar Artistry — Martin Taylor (Ashley Mark Publishing)
**Credit:** Martin Taylor, transcribed by Colin Oxley. "Jazz Guitar Artistry Vol 1" (Ashley Mark Jazz Series). From the recording "A Tribute to Art Tatum" (CLP 077).
**Maps to:** Jazz Branch → Jazz Standards, Technique

### Songs transcribed
- Don't Get Around Much Anymore
- I've Got The World On A String
- Old Man River
- It's Only A Paper Moon
- Takin' A Chance On Love
- Time After Time
- All The Things You Are

### Key concepts
- **Solo guitar interpretation** — melody, harmony, bass lines simultaneously
- **Extended chords** — 9ths, 11ths, 13ths (Gmaj13, Ab6/9, G#13)
- **Altered dominants** — C13, D7#9
- **Diminished harmony** — passing diminished chords
- **Rubato playing** — expressive rhythmic freedom
- **Articulations** — falls, bends, sustain, staccato
- **Note-for-note transcription approach** — exact fingering, position markings (Roman numerals), finger numbers

### Learning approach
- Study the masters (Art Tatum) by learning exact transcriptions
- Solo guitar = whole band on one instrument
- Position-specific fingering develops fretboard mastery

## 7. Complete Guitar Series Vol 1 — Cary White (Wildwood Publishing)
**Credit:** Cary White, "The Complete Guitar Series, Volume 1" (Wildwood Publishing)
**Note:** File was named Bruce Arnold but content is by Cary White
**Maps to:** Foundation node, Doing → Drills

### Table of Contents
- Philosophy of Guitar (hybrid classical + popular)
- Guitar Parts
- Fingerings (PIMA system, rest strokes, free strokes)
- Tuning the Guitar
- Chords and Chord Songs
- Notes on Strings 1–3, then 4–6
- Practice Routines
- Instrumental Pieces
- Basic and Various Scales
- Finger Picking and Strum Patterns
- Tablature Rock Songs
- Flash Cards

### Learning progression
1. Foundation — instrument anatomy, posture, tuning
2. Right hand mechanics — rest strokes, free strokes
3. Left hand dexterity — chromatic 0-1-2-3-4 exercise
4. String literacy — treble strings first, then bass
5. Integration — instrumental pieces, strum patterns, finger picking
6. Tablature for rock songs (separated from classical note reading)
7. Flash cards for memorisation

### Key concepts
- Hybrid classical + popular approach
- PIMA finger system (P=thumb, I=index, M=middle, A=ring)
- Rest stroke (Apoyando) vs Free stroke (Tirando)
- Fretboard navigation via dot markers (3, 5, 7, 9, 12)
- String name mnemonic: "Eat Another Nut Get Bigger Everytime"

## 9. Ear Training Manual for Musicians — US Navy (NAVEDTRA 10243, 1986)
**Credit:** U.S. Army / Naval Education and Training Professional Development and Technology Center
**Maps to:** Ear Training node, Reference Library

### Complete structure
- Ch 1: Simple time & basic harmony (divided beat, major triad in numeral notation)
- Ch 2: Compound time & staff notation (divided beat in 6/8/9/8, major triad on staff)
- Ch 3: Subdivided beats & minor harmony (16th notes, minor triads)
- Ch 4: Compound subdivision & scales (complex meters, major/minor scale ear training)
- Ch 5: Syncopation & interval/triad recognition
- Ch 6: Basic melodic ear training (diatonic, leaps of 3rds)
- Ch 7: Intermediate melodic (subdivided beat, 3rds and 4ths)
- Ch 8: Advanced melodic (syncopation, leaps up to octaves)
- Ch 9: Chromatic/non-diatonic melodies

### Appendices
- Time beating patterns (conducting)
- Counting system (1&2&, 1e&a)
- Performance procedures
- Performance guidelines

### Military training philosophy (maps to our learning tech)
- Highly structured, progressive, skill-specific
- Incremental complexity (simple time → compound → syncopation → chromatic)
- Standardised counting methods
- Performance-focused (not just theory)

## 11. How to Play Jazz and Improvise Vol 1 — Jamey Aebersold
**Credit:** Jamey Aebersold, "How to Play Jazz and Improvise, Volume 1" Revised 6th Ed (©1967, revised ©1992)
**Maps to:** Jazz Branch → core curriculum, Play → Jazz Standards

### Core concepts
- **Right Brain / Left Brain balance** — technical knowledge (left) + creative spontaneity (right)
- **Scales and Chords as vocabulary** — every chord symbol = both a chord AND a scale
- **Sing before you play** — internalise the changes vocally first
- **Improvisation is natural** — not mystical, built on specific musical devices

### Theory topics
- Eighth-note exercises and swing
- The Bebop Scale
- Pentatonic Scale and its use
- Chromaticism
- Playing the Blues
- Blues Scale
- Seventh Chords
- Melodic Development — Tension and Release
- Related Scales and Modes
- Articulations
- Scale Syllabus (comprehensive reference)
- Dominant Seventh Tree of Scale Choices

### Play-along structure
- Transposed for Concert, Bb, Eb, and Bass Clef instruments
- 20 musical examples per transposition
- Blues melodies
- Practice procedure: Listen → Sing → Play → Improvise

### Practice methodology
- Don't rush — knowledge ≠ application
- Memorise scales, get eyes off the page
- Main objective is to IMPROVISE, not drill exercises
- 12-bar blues as foundation form
